## Field Definitions & Basic Properties

- **Definition**: **Field** $F$: Nontrivial commutative ring; every non-zero element is a **unit**.[^def5.26]
    - Notation: $\langle F; +, -, 0, \cdot, 1 \rangle$.
    - Division possible by any non-zero element.
- **Integral Domain**: Every field has **no zero-divisors** ($ab=0 \implies a=0 \lor b=0$).[^thm5.24]
- **Prime Fields**: Smallest subfield contained in a field (no proper subfields).
    - $\mathbb{Q}$: Prime field of characteristic 0.
    - **$\mathbb{Z}_p$** ($GF(p)$): Integers modulo $p$.
        - Field **if and only if** $p$ is **prime**.[^thm5.23]
        - $m$ composite $\implies$ $\mathbb{Z}_m$ has zero-divisors (not a field).

## Finite Fields (Galois Fields)

- **Classification by Size**:
    - **Existence**: Finite field of size $q$ exists **iff** $q = p^n$ (prime power).[^thm5.39]
        - Irreducible polynomials of degree $d \ge 1$ exist for every prime $p$.[^thm5.38]
    - **Uniqueness**: All fields of size $q$ are **isomorphic** (structurally identical).[^thm5.39]
    - **Notation**: $GF(q)$ or $GF(p^n)$.
- **Multiplicative Structure**:
    - Group $GF(q)^*$ (non-zero elements) is **cyclic**.[^thm5.40]
    - Generator also called **Primitive Element**.
    - *Example*: $x$ in $GF(4)^*$ generates group: $x^1=x, x^2=x+1, x^3=1$.

## Construction of Extension Fields

**Goal**: Construct field of size $q = p^d$ by extending base $F = GF(p)$.

- **Quotient Structure $F[x]_{m(x)}$**:
    - Set of polynomials in $F[x]$ with degree $< d = \deg(m(x))$.[^def5.34]
    - **Cardinality**: $|F|^d$ elements ($q^d$ if $|F|=q$).[^lem5.34]
- **Arithmetic Rules**:
    - **Congruence**: Equivalence modulo $m(x)$ (difference divisible by $m(x)$).[^lem5.33]
    - **Ring Structure**: Ring under polynomial addition/multiplication modulo $m(x)$.[^lem5.35]
        - *Addition*: Component-wise (modulo $p$).
        - *Multiplication*: Standard product, reduced by remainder of division by $m(x)$.
- **Field Condition**:
    - $F[x]_{m(x)}$ is a **Field** **iff** modulus $m(x)$ is **irreducible** over $F$.[^thm5.37]
    - Reducible $m(x)$ $\implies$ zero-divisors exist.
- **Inverses**:
    - Element $a(x)$ invertible iff $\gcd(a(x), m(x)) = 1$.[^lem5.36]
    - Irreducible $m(x)$ $\implies$ all non-zero $a(x)$ invertible $\implies$ Field.
    - *Computation*: Extended Euclidean Algorithm for polynomials.
- **Examples**:
    - **$\mathbb{C}$**: $\mathbb{R}[x]_{x^2+1}$.
        - $x^2+1$ irreducible over $\mathbb{R}$.
        - $x^2 \equiv -1$ (isomorphic to $i$).
    - **$GF(4)$**: $GF(2)[x]_{x^2+x+1}$.
        - $x^2+x+1$ irreducible over $GF(2)$.
        - Elements: $\{0, 1, x, x+1\}$.
        - Calc: $(x+1)x = x^2+x \equiv (x+1)+x = 1$ ($x^2 \equiv x+1$).

[^def5.26]: **Definition 5.26.** A *field* is a nontrivial commutative ring $F$ in which every nonzero element is a unit, i.e., $F^* = F \setminus \{0\}$.
[^thm5.24]: **Theorem 5.24.** A *field* is an *integral domain*.
[^thm5.23]: **Theorem 5.23.** $\mathbb{Z}_p$ is a field if and only if $p$ is prime.
[^thm5.39]: **Theorem 5.39.** There exists a finite field with $q$ elements if and only if $q$ is a power of a prime. Moreover, any two finite fields of the same size $q$ are isomorphic.
[^thm5.38]: **Theorem 5.38.** For every prime $p$ and every $d \ge 1$ there exists an irreducible polynomial of degree $d$ in $GF(p)[x]$. In particular, there exists a finite field with $p^d$ elements.
[^thm5.40]: **Theorem 5.40.** The multiplicative group of every finite field $GF(q)$ is cyclic.
[^def5.34]: **Definition 5.34.** Let $m(x)$ be a polynomial of degree $d$ over $F$. Then $F[x]_{m(x)} \stackrel{\text{def}}{=} \{a(x) \in F[x] \mid \deg(a(x)) < d\}$.
[^lem5.34]: **Lemma 5.34.** Let $F$ be a finite field with $q$ elements and let $m(x)$ be a polynomial of degree $d$ over $F$. Then $|F[x]_{m(x)}| = q^d$.
[^lem5.33]: **Lemma 5.33.** Congruence modulo $m(x)$ is an equivalence relation on $F[x]$, and each equivalence class has a unique representative of degree less than $\deg(m(x))$.
[^lem5.35]: **Lemma 5.35.** $F[x]_{m(x)}$ is a ring with respect to addition and multiplication modulo $m(x)$.
[^thm5.37]: **Theorem 5.37.** The ring $F[x]_{m(x)}$ is a field if and only if $m(x)$ is irreducible.
[^lem5.36]: **Lemma 5.36.** The congruence equation $a(x)b(x) \equiv_{m(x)} 1$ (for a given $a(x)$) has a solution $b(x) \in F[x]_{m(x)}$ if and only if $\text{gcd}(a(x), m(x)) = 1$. The solution is unique.
