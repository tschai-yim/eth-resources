- **Mathematical Statement (Proposition):** Unambiguously **true or false** according to mathematical laws.

> **Definition 2.1.** *A mathematical statement (also called proposition) is a statement that is true or false in an absolute, indisputable sense, according to the laws of mathematics.*

- **Types of Statements:**
    - **Theorem:** Important true statement.
    - **Lemma:** Intermediate true statement, aids in proving a theorem.
    - **Corollary:** Simple consequence of a theorem or lemma.
    - **Conjecture:** Believed to be true, but unproven (e.g., Goldbach Conjecture).
    - **Assumption:** Assumed true for a line of reasoning.
- **Concept of a Proof:**
    - **Purpose:** Demonstrate a statement is true.
    - **Informal Definition:** Sequence of simple, verifiable steps from axioms and known facts to the target statement.

> **Definition 2.2.** *(Informal.) A proof of a statement $S$ is a sequence of simple, easily verifiable, consecutive steps. The proof starts from a set of axioms (things postulated to be true) and known (previously proved) facts. Each step corresponds to the application of a derivation rule to a few already proven statements, resulting in a newly proved statement, until the final step results in $S$.*

- **Proof Strategies:** more in [[2.6 Proof Patterns]]
    - **Valid (Modus Ponens):** To prove statement $S$:
        1. Find a suitable statement $R$.
        2. Prove $R$ is true.
        3. Prove the implication $R \Rightarrow S$.
    - **Invalid:** **Never start with the conclusion** and derive a known truth.
        - **Reason:** A false premise can imply anything ($F \Rightarrow T$ is a true implication).
        - **Example Flaw:** Assuming $1 < 0$ (false) $\Rightarrow$ adding 1 gives $2 < 1$ (false). This doesn't prove the assumption.
- **Levels of Proof:**
    - **Proof Sketch/Idea:** Main, non-obvious ideas without full detail.
    - **Complete Proof:** Each step justified by definitions or rules.
    - **Formal Proof:** Phrased entirely in a formal system (proof calculus).
