## Polynomial Ring Fundamentals ($R[x]$)

- **Definition**: **Polynomial** over commutative ring $R$: formal sum $a(x) = \sum_{i=0}^d a_i x^i$ ($a_i \in R$).[^def5.25]
    - $R[x]$: Set of all polynomials over $R$.
- **Degree** ($\text{deg}(a(x))$): Highest index $i$ such that $a_i \neq 0$.[^def5.25]
    - **Zero Polynomial**: Defined as $\text{deg}(0) = -\infty$.
    - **Arithmetic**: $\text{deg}(f \cdot g) = \text{deg}(f) + \text{deg}(g)$ (in Integral Domains).[^lem5.22]
        - *Note*: Fails in non-domains (e.g., $\mathbb{Z}_4[x]$: $2x \cdot 2x = 0$).
- **Algebraic Structure**:
    - $R[x]$ is a **commutative ring** if $R$ is commutative.[^thm5.21]
    - $D[x]$ is an **Integral Domain** iff $D$ is one.[^lem5.22]
- **Units**: Exactly units of base ring $D$ ($D[x]^* = D^*$, constant polynomials).[^lem5.22]
    - *Example*: $\mathbb{Z}[x]$ units: $\pm 1$. $F[x]$ units: $F \setminus \{0\}$.

## Arithmetic in $F[x]$ (Division & GCD)

- **Monic Polynomial**: Leading coefficient is $1$.[^def5.27]
    - *Note*: Analog to "positive integers" for normalization.
- **Associates**: $a \sim b$ if $a = u \cdot b$ for unit $u$.[^def5.30]
    - In $F[x]$, every polynomial associates with exactly one **monic** polynomial.[^lem5.26]
- **Polynomial Division**: For $a(x), b(x) \neq 0$, unique quotient $q(x)$ and remainder $r(x)$ exist:[^thm5.25]
    $$a(x) = b(x) \cdot q(x) + r(x) \quad \text{and} \quad \text{deg}(r(x)) < \text{deg}(b(x))$$
    - *Algorithm*: Repeatedly subtract multiples of divisor to eliminate leading term.
    - *Tip*: In $GF(p)$, calculate coefficients modulo $p$.
- **Greatest Common Divisor (GCD)**:
    - **Definition**: Monic polynomial of highest degree dividing both $a$ and $b$.[^def5.29]
    - **Euclidean Domain**: $F[x]$ allows Euclidean Algorithm for GCD computation.[^def5.32]

## Roots and Interpolation

- **Evaluation**: Ring homomorphism $a(x) \mapsto a(\alpha)$ mapping $x$ to $\alpha \in F$.[^lem5.28]
- **Roots**: Element $\alpha \in F$ where $a(\alpha) = 0$.[^def5.33]
- **Linear Factors**:
    - $\alpha$ is a root of $a(x)$ $\iff$ $(x - \alpha)$ divides $a(x)$.[^lem5.29]
    - *Application*: Check divisibility by $(x-c)$ via evaluation at $c$.
- **Root Bounds**: Non-zero polynomial of degree $d$ has **at most** $d$ roots.[^thm5.31]
    - *Example*: $x^p - x$ in $GF(p)$ has exactly $p$ roots (all elements).
- **Lagrange Interpolation**:
    - Polynomial of degree $\le d$ **uniquely determined** by $d+1$ points $(\alpha_i, \beta_i)$.[^lem5.32]
    - **Formula**: $$a(x) = \sum_{i=1}^{d+1} \beta_i \cdot \frac{\prod_{j \neq i} (x - \alpha_j)}{\prod_{j \neq i} (\alpha_i - \alpha_j)}$$

## Irreducibility & Factorization

- **Definition**: **Irreducible** polynomial ($\text{deg} \ge 1$) only divisible by constants and constant multiples of itself.[^def5.28]
    - Analog: Prime numbers in $\mathbb{Z}$.
    - **Reducible**: Factors into polynomials of lower degree.
- **Irreducibility Tests**:
    - **Degree 1**: Always irreducible.
    - **Degree 2 & 3**: Irreducible $\iff$ **no roots** in $F$.[^cor5.30]
        - *Reasoning*: Reducible implies factor of degree 1 (linear) $\implies$ root exists.
    - **Degree $\ge 4$**: No roots is *necessary* but **not sufficient**.
        - *Tip*: Must check for irreducible factors up to degree $d/2$.
- **Unique Factorization**: Every polynomial factors uniquely (up to order/units) into irreducible polynomials.[^thm5.27]

[^def5.25]: **Definition 5.25.** A *polynomial* $a(x)$ over a commutative ring $R$ in the indeterminate $x$ is a formal expression of the form $$a(x) = a_d x^d + a_{d-1}x^{d-1} + \dots + a_1 x + a_0 = \sum_{i=0}^d a_i x^i$$ for some non-negative integer $d$, with $a_i \in R$. The *degree* of $a(x)$, denoted $\text{deg}(a(x))$, is the greatest $i$ for which $a_i \neq 0$. The special polynomial 0 (i.e., all the $a_i$ are 0) is defined to have degree "minus infinity". Let $R[x]$ denote the set of polynomials (in $x$) over $R$.
[^lem5.22]: **Lemma 5.22.** Let $D$ be an integral domain. Then (i) $D[x]$ is an integral domain. (ii) The degree of the product of two polynomial is the sum of their degrees. (iii) The units of $D[x]$ are the constant polynomials that are units of $D$: $D[x]^* = D^*$.
[^thm5.21]: **Theorem 5.21.** For any commutative ring $R$, $R[x]$ is a commutative ring.
[^def5.27]: **Definition 5.27.** A polynomial $a(x) \in F[x]$ is called *monic* if the leading coefficient is 1.
[^def5.30]: **Definition 5.30.** In an integral domain, $a$ and $b$ are called *associates*, denoted $a \sim b$, if $a = ub$ for some unit $u$.
[^lem5.26]: **Lemma 5.26.** $a \sim b \iff a \mid b \land b \mid a$.
[^thm5.25]: **Theorem 5.25.** Let $F$ be a field. For any $a(x)$ and $b(x) \neq 0$ in $F[x]$ there exist a unique $q(x)$ (the quotient) and a unique $r(x)$ (the remainder) such that $$a(x) = b(x) \cdot q(x) + r(x) \quad \text{and} \quad \text{deg}(r(x)) < \text{deg}(b(x)).$$
[^def5.29]: **Definition 5.29.** The monic polynomial $g(x)$ of largest degree such that $g(x) \mid a(x)$ and $g(x) \mid b(x)$ is called *the greatest common divisor* of $a(x)$ and $b(x)$, denoted $\text{gcd}(a(x), b(x))$.
[^def5.32]: **Definition 5.32.** A *Euclidean domain* is an integral domain $D$ together with a so-called *degree function* $d : D \setminus \{0\} \to \mathbb{N}$ such that (i) For every $a$ and $b \neq 0$ in $D$ there exist $q$ and $r$ such that $a = bq + r$ and $d(r) < d(b)$ or $r = 0$. (ii) For all nonzero $a$ and $b$ in $D$, $d(a) \le d(ab)$.
[^lem5.28]: **Lemma 5.28.** Polynomial evaluation is compatible with the ring operations: • If $c(x) = a(x) + b(x)$, then $c(\alpha) = a(\alpha) + b(\alpha)$ for any $\alpha$. • If $c(x) = a(x) \cdot b(x)$, then $c(\alpha) = a(\alpha) \cdot b(\alpha)$ for any $\alpha$.
[^def5.33]: **Definition 5.33.** Let $a(x) \in R[x]$. An element $\alpha \in R$ for which $a(\alpha) = 0$ is called a *root* of $a(x)$.
[^lem5.29]: **Lemma 5.29.** For a field $F$, $\alpha \in F$ is a root of $a(x)$ if and only if $x - \alpha$ divides $a(x)$.
[^thm5.31]: **Theorem 5.31.** For a field $F$, a nonzero polynomial $a(x) \in F[x]$ of degree $d$ has at most $d$ roots.
[^lem5.32]: **Lemma 5.32.** A polynomial $a(x) \in F[x]$ of degree at most $d$ is uniquely determined by any $d+1$ values of $a(x)$, i.e., by $a(\alpha_1), \dots, a(\alpha_{d+1})$ for any distinct $\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_{d+1} \in F$.
[^def5.28]: **Definition 5.28.** A polynomial $a(x) \in F[x]$ with degree at least 1 is called *irreducible* if it is divisible only by constant polynomials and by constant multiples of $a(x)$.
[^cor5.30]: **Corollary 5.30.** A polynomial $a(x)$ of degree 2 or 3 over a field $F$ is irreducible if and only if it has no root.
[^thm5.27]: **Theorem 5.27.** In a Euclidean domain every element can be factored uniquely (up to taking associates) into irreducible elements.
