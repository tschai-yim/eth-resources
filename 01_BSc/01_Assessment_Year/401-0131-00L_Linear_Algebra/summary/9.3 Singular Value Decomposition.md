## Definition & Geometry

- **Singular Value Decomposition (SVD)**: Generalizes eigendecomposition to **any** matrix $A \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}$ (non-square, non-symmetric).
- **The Decomposition**: $A = U\Sigma V^\top$ .[^def9.3.1]
    - **$U \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times m}$**: Orthogonal matrix ($U^\top U = I$). Columns = **Left Singular Vectors**.
    - **$V \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$**: Orthogonal matrix ($V^\top V = I$). Columns = **Right Singular Vectors**.
    - **$\Sigma \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}$**: Rectangular **diagonal matrix** with entries $\sigma_i \ge 0$.
- **Singular Values ($\sigma_i$)**:
    - Diagonal entries $\Sigma_{ii}$.
    - **Ordered**: $\sigma_1 \ge \sigma_2 \ge \dots \ge \sigma_{\min} \ge 0$ .[^def9.3.1]
    - **Rank**: Number of non-zero $\sigma_i$ equals **rank** $r$ .[^rem9.3.2]
- **Geometric Interpretation**: Any linear map decomposes into: **Rotation** ($V^\top$) $\to$ **Stretch** along axes ($\Sigma$) $\to$ **Rotation** ($U$).
- **Diagonalizing Basis**: Transformation is diagonal when input basis is $V$ and output basis is $U$ .[^thm9.3.3]

## Compact vs. Full SVD

- **Compact SVD**: Efficient storage for rank $r$ .[^rem9.3.2]
    - Form: $A = U_r \Sigma_r V_r^\top$.
    - **Dimensions**: $U_r \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times r}$, $\Sigma_r \in \mathbb{R}^{r \times r}$ (strictly positive diagonal), $V_r \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times r}$.
    - **Savings**: Stores $r(m+n+1)$ instead of $mn$ entries.
- **Construction: Compact $\to$ Full SVD**:
    - **Singular Values**: Append zeros to diagonal of $\Sigma$ until size $m \times n$.
    - **Left Basis ($U$)**:
        - $U_r$ contains first $r$ columns (Basis for $C(A)$).
        - Extend to orthonormal basis of $\mathbb{R}^m$ (e.g., Gram-Schmidt).
        - Added vectors span **$N(A^\top)$** (orthogonal complement of col space).
    - **Right Basis ($V$)**:
        - $V_r$ contains first $r$ columns (Basis for $C(A^\top)$).
        - Extend to orthonormal basis of $\mathbb{R}^n$.
        - Added vectors span **$N(A)$** (orthogonal complement of row space).

## Connection to Spectral Theory

- **Derivation via Symmetric Matrices**:
    - **$V$**: Eigenvectors of symmetric matrix **$A^\top A$**.
    - **$U$**: Eigenvectors of symmetric matrix **$AA^\top$**.
    - **$\Sigma$**: $\sigma_i = \sqrt{\lambda_i}$ (Square root of non-zero eigenvalues of $A^\top A$).
- **Construction Warning**:
    - **Cannot** choose $U$ and $V$ independently (signs might mismatch).
    - **Procedure**:
        1. Compute $V_r$ and $\Sigma_r$ from $A^\top A$.
        2. **Force** $U_r$ via relation $U_r = A V_r \Sigma_r^{-1}$ (or vice versa).
        3. Extend to full bases if needed.

## The "Big Picture": Unifying Concepts

- **Fundamental Subspaces** (Explicit Bases):
    - **Column Space ($C(A)$)**: Spanned by first $r$ columns of $U$.
    - **Left Nullspace ($N(A^\top)$)**: Spanned by last $m-r$ columns of $U$.
    - **Row Space ($C(A^\top)$)**: Spanned by first $r$ columns of $V$.
    - **Nullspace ($N(A)$)**: Spanned by last $n-r$ columns of $V$.
- **Generalizing Inverses (Pseudoinverse)**:
    - **Moore-Penrose Pseudoinverse ($A^\dagger$)**: Unified formula for **all** rank cases (full column, full row, or singular).
    - Formula: **$A^\dagger = V \Sigma^\dagger U^\top$**.
    - Operation: Invert non-zero $\sigma_i$ ($1/\sigma_i$), transpose $\Sigma$, swap $U/V$.
- **Relation to Eigendecomposition**:
    - If $A$ is symmetric **Positive Semi-Definite (PSD)**: SVD $\equiv$ Eigendecomposition ($U=V$, $\sigma_i = \lambda_i$).
    - If $A$ is symmetric (general): $U$ and $V$ are eigenvectors, but signs differ for negative eigenvalues ($\sigma_i = |\lambda_i|$).

## Applications

- **Rank-r Decomposition**:
    - Sum of outer products of singular vectors .[^prop9.3.4]
    - Formula: $A = \sum_{k=1}^r \sigma_k u_k v_k^\top$.
    - **Low-Rank Approximation**: Truncating this sum (keeping largest $\sigma_k$) gives best approximation (e.g., image compression).

[^def9.3.1]: **Definition 9.3.1 (SVD — Singular Value Decomposition).** Let $A \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}$. There exist orthogonal matrices $U \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times m}$ and $V \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$ such that $$(39) \quad A = U\Sigma V^\top,$$ where $\Sigma \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}$ is a diagonal matrix, in the sense that $\Sigma_{ij} = 0$ when $i \neq j$, and the diagonal elements are non-negative and ordered in descending order. $U^\top U = I$ and $V^\top V = I$. The columns $u_1, \dots, u_m$ of $U$ are called the left singular vectors of $A$ and are orthonormal. The columns $v_1, \dots, v_n$ of $V$ are called the right singular vectors of $A$ and are orthonormal. The diagonal elements of $\Sigma$, $\sigma_i = \Sigma_{ii}$ are called the singular values of $A$ and are ordered as $$\sigma_1 \ge \dots \ge \sigma_{\min\{m, n\}}.$$
[^rem9.3.2]: **Remark 9.3.2.** If $A$ has rank $r$ we can write the SVD in a more compact form: $$(40) \quad A = U_r \Sigma_r V_r^\top,$$ where $U_r \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times r}$ contains the first $r$ left singular vectors, $V_r \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times r}$ contains the first $r$ right singular vectors and $\Sigma_r \in \mathbb{R}^{r \times r}$ is a diagonal matrix with the first $r$ singular values. Notice that storing such a decomposition in the computer requires storing $r \times (m + n + 1)$ real numbers rather than $m \times n$ real numbers which would be required to store $A$ naively. When a matrix has small rank these are crucial savings. Taking this one step forward, when a matrix is well approximated by a low rank matrix, oftentimes one stores only a small rank approximation of a matrix $A$. This is a crucial idea in tasks ranging from Image Compressions, Numerical Analysis, and Machine Learning. Oftentimes the subscript is omitted and the compact SVD is simply written as $U\Sigma V^\top$ while specifying the dimensions of the matrices involved to specify which form of the SVD is being considered.


[^thm9.3.3]: **Theorem 9.3.3.** Every matrix $A \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}$ has an SVD decomposition of the form (39). In other words: **Every linear transformation is diagonal when viewed in the bases of the singular vectors.**
[^prop9.3.4]: **Proposition 9.3.4.** Let $A \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}$ be a matrix with rank $r$. Let $\sigma_1, \dots, \sigma_r$ be the non-zero singular values of $A$, $u_1, \dots, u_r$ the corresponding left singular vectors and $v_1, \dots, v_r$ the corresponding right singular vectors. Then $$(42) \quad A = \sum_{k=1}^r \sigma_k u_k v_k^\top.$$
