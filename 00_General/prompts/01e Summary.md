## Formatting Requirements

- Use **bullet points** or **very short phrases** and **avoid prose**.
- Use **indented bullet points** for sub-items/lists as needed. (indent using 4 spaces)
- Highlight **important keywords** and all mentioned **terms** in bold (`**Word**`).
- Formulate **specifically and concretely**, adding examples where possible (e.g., in parentheses or as a sub-item).
- The language should be **concise, clear, and understandable** (US English).
- Prefer **simple and common language**. Avoid unnecessary technical terms that are not explicitly mentioned in the learning objectives or sources and can be easily replaced by more common words (e.g., "dehydration" instead of the less common "desiccation").
- **Avoid redundancies**: Do not repeat information verbatim or very similarly in directly consecutive sections. Short references or applying a concept as an example in a different context (e.g., mentioning climate factors in the climate section and again as an example for animal adaptations) are acceptable if they aid understanding.
- The goal is a structure that allows for quick comprehension and review (**Anki-friendly**).
- Mark subtitles as Markdown headings (`## Subtitle`) and do not number them.
- Do **not** reference the attachments in the text, as they will not be available to the reader later.
- Prefer inline LaTeX (`$<expression>$`, not wrapped in backticks, Obsidian syntax) for mathematical expressions. Only use blocks for big formulas.
- Only use syntax that works well in a standard installation of Obsidian. Meaning among other things no quotes or tables on indented levels (like lists).
